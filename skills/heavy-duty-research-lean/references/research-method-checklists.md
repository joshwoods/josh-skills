# Research Method Checklists

Use these checklists only when the task needs methodology support. They are not topic evidence.

## Source Credibility

Classify source confidence by role and limitations:

- **High**: primary source, official record, peer-reviewed paper, reproducible dataset, source code, or directly observed artifact.
- **Medium**: reputable expert analysis, transparent trade reporting, well-cited technical writing, or synthesis with named sources.
- **Low**: unsourced posts, marketing copy, anonymous commentary, SEO summaries, unverifiable claims, or stale summaries.

Ask:

- Who created the source, and what incentive or constraint shapes it?
- Is the claim directly observed, measured, interpreted, or repeated from elsewhere?
- Is the date appropriate for the claim?
- Can an important claim be traced to a primary source?
- What would this source be weak at proving?

## Evidence Synthesis

Use this structure for high-rigor work:

1. Break the question into claims that can be supported or falsified.
2. Map each claim to the strongest available evidence.
3. Separate agreement, disagreement, and missing evidence.
4. Grade confidence based on source quality, independence, recency, and consistency.
5. Explain what evidence would change the conclusion.

Avoid source piles. Convert evidence into a decision, tradeoff, or next action.

## Literature Discovery

For academic or technical literature:

- Start with review papers, standards, canonical papers, or official docs.
- Follow citation chains backward for foundations and forward for recent developments.
- Prefer papers with clear methods, accessible data/code, and independent replication.
- Track population, setting, benchmark, or implementation assumptions before generalizing.
- Separate a paper's measured result from the paper's interpretation of that result.

## Educational References

Choose learning materials that teach the user's topic, not generic research process:

- **Beginner**: official overviews, textbooks, primers, explainers from authoritative institutions.
- **Intermediate**: standards, handbooks, review papers, practitioner guides with transparent methods.
- **Advanced**: original papers, datasets, source code, filings, regulatory text, technical specs.

Give a reading order when the topic is broad. Omit educational references when they would distract from a narrow decision.
